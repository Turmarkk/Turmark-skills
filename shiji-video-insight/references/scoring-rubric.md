# Scoring Rubric

Score each candidate out of 100 before deciding whether to expand it.

## Dimensions

### 1. Anomaly Strength - 30

How strongly does the topic trigger "this is not normal"?

- 25-30: immediately strange, clear tension
- 15-24: interesting but not instantly sharp
- 0-14: mostly ordinary narrative

### 2. Evidence Density - 20

Can the topic support a chain instead of a single fragment?

- 16-20: direct evidence plus comparison and support
- 8-15: enough for a compact argument
- 0-7: thin and unstable

### 3. Explainability - 15

Can a general history audience follow the logic?

- 12-15: explainable in clear, vivid terms
- 6-11: understandable with effort
- 0-5: too abstract or too technical

### 4. Distribution Potential - 20

Does it naturally become a title or hook?

- 16-20: strong click sentence appears naturally
- 8-15: works with some packaging effort
- 0-7: low tension or low curiosity

### 5. Series Potential - 15

Can it connect to a repeatable format or channel identity?

- 12-15: strong recurring column or series potential
- 6-11: one-off but still useful
- 0-5: isolated curiosity

## Thresholds

- 85-100: Priority lead topic
- 70-84: Strong topic, expand with care
- 55-69: Backup topic or part of a larger collection
- Below 55: Do not force into a full script

## Hard Gates

Do not enter `L3 Script Mode` if any of these are missing:

- at least one comparison group
- a main explanation plus alternatives
- an explicit evidence boundary

Even a high-scoring topic should stop at `L2` if it fails any hard gate.

## Fast Decision Rule

Do not expand to full script if both of these are true:

- anomaly strength below 20
- evidence density below 12

That combination usually means the topic is catchy but too weak.
